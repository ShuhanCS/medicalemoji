# Kidney v2.3.0 Regression Audit

This review compares the requested July 26 paired proposal with `v2.2.2`. It is internal and is not
part of the Unicode-facing PDF.

## Restored from the July 26 proposal

- Paired-kidney imagery at both required sizes, including the polished 72x72 rendering.
- Matching language about inward-facing forms, medial notches, diagonal offset, vessels, and vendor
  variation.
- The explicit image-rights certification and CC0 dedication. `v2.2.2` retained only a general
  originality statement and dropped the express ownership, CC0, and Unicode-license grant language.
- Concrete sequence examples for `kidney stone` and `kidney tests` with medical references.
- Explicit practitioner evidence that the Beans substitution is confusing in kidney-disease use.
- U.S. and U.K. patient-guidance examples for testing, dialysis, transplantation, and donation.

## Preserved from v2.2.2

- Final ten-person submitter list and exact requested order.
- Lauren Beaudin immediately after Timur Erk.
- Heena Purohit before David Rhew.
- Jarone Lee excluded.
- No medical credential attached to Timur Erk and no stale company affiliations attached to David
  Rhew or Heena Purohit.

## Not carried into the requested proposal

- `v2.2.2` verifies that the CLDR English annotation data routes `kidney` only to U+1FAD8 BEANS and
  uses that mismatch under Breaks New Ground and Compatibility. The July 26 proposal predates that
  argument and says Compatibility is not applicable.
- `v2.2.2` includes a dedicated native-size comparator board. The July 26 proposal relies on its
  purpose-built 18x18 examples and deterministic validation instead.
- `v2.2.2` uses shorter tables for sequence examples. The July 26 proposal uses sourced prose and
  named phrases.

## Filing recommendation

Review `v2.3.0` first as the requested faithful paired-art restoration. Before filing, consider a
new synchronized packet that adds the verified CLDR compatibility argument without changing the
paired artwork, author roster, or the stronger sourced use-case language.
