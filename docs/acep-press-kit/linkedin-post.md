A small piece of news I'm genuinely proud of: the **American College of Emergency
Physicians** has endorsed the Medical Emoji Project. 🫀

A few years ago, our team helped bring the anatomical heart and lungs into the Unicode
Standard. They now appear on billions of devices — and that work showed something
simple: the symbols people use every day can do real clinical work, helping patients
and clinicians communicate across languages, literacy levels, and moments of crisis.

We're now pursuing ten more concepts — liver, kidney, stomach, intestine, white blood
cell, ECG, spine, blood products, pill packs, and a weight scale. ACEP's letter names
every one of them.

As an emergency physician, this means a lot. Clear communication under pressure is the
whole job, and these symbols are meant to add a familiar, global layer for patient
education, discharge instructions, and public health — never to replace clinical
judgment.

Grateful to ACEP, and to everyone who has supported this along the way.

See the concepts and every endorsement → medicalemoji.org/endorsements
