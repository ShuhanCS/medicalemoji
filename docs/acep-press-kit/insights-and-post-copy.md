# Medical Emoji — Insights & Post Kit for ACEP

Prepared for the American College of Emergency Physicians communications team to
accompany a post — presented by the **ACEP Emergency Medicine Informatics Section** —
about ACEP's June 5, 2026 letter of support for the Medical Emoji Project. Everything
here is drafted to be lifted directly or adapted to ACEP's voice.

**The drive-home point:** ACEP is the first national, multi-specialty physician
society to support the entire Medical Emoji effort at once — its letter of support
names all ten concepts now in development. The emoji aim to give clinicians and patients a
familiar, globally available visual layer for organ systems, infection, cardiac
monitoring, transfusion, medication use, and weight-based care.

---

## 1. Background (2–3 sentences for the post)

The Medical Emoji Project advocates for adding high-utility medical symbols to the
Unicode Standard, the global system behind the emoji on every phone. In 2019,
emergency physician **Shuhan He, MD** (Massachusetts General Hospital) and
collaborators helped win Unicode approval for the **anatomical heart 🫀 and lungs
🫁**, which shipped on billions of devices in 2021 and were documented in *JAMA*.
The project is now pursuing ten further concepts — and ACEP has now written a letter
in support of that work.

## 2. Why ACEP's letter of support matters

- It is a **clinical and professional letter of support from a national society**, the kind
  of broad-applicability signal Unicode weighs when evaluating proposals.
- It covers **all ten current concepts at once**, rather than a single organ — a first
  for the project.
- It frames medical emoji where emergency medicine lives: **clear communication under
  time pressure, across languages, literacy levels, ages, and disabilities** — for
  patient education, discharge instructions, public health messaging, and medical
  education.
- ACEP is explicit that these symbols **add to**, and do not replace, clinical
  language or professional interpretation.

## 3. The ten concepts ACEP named (with a clinical hook for each)

Use any subset. Each pairs a concept with a fact that lands quickly.

| Concept | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Liver** | Liver disease causes ~2 million deaths a year worldwide; ~25% of Americans have non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. |
| **Kidney** | Chronic kidney disease affects ~10% of the global population. |
| **Stomach** | GERD carries an estimated $18B in direct and $75B in indirect costs annually. |
| **Intestine / Bowel** | Colorectal cancer is the 3rd most diagnosed cancer and 2nd leading cause of cancer death. |
| **White Blood Cell** | Infection and immunity shaped history from the Black Plague to COVID-19; 15.5M U.S. primary-care visits for infectious disease in 2016. |
| **ECG / EKG** | One person dies every 36 seconds from cardiovascular disease in the U.S. |
| **Spine** | Low back pain is the #1 cause of years lived with disability worldwide. |
| **Blood Product** | Every 2 seconds, someone in the U.S. needs blood. |
| **Pill Pack** | ~50,000 opioid overdose deaths in the U.S. in 2019. |
| **Weight Scale** | Over 2 billion people worldwide live with overweight or obesity. |

## 4. How Unicode emoji approval works (one line of context)

Unicode reviews each proposal against published criteria — distinctiveness, expected
usage, image licensing, and broad applicability — through a formal annual process.
Society support speaks to clinical relevance and broad applicability.
