# How to write an emoji proposal that wins

Derived from **every proposal written since 2020 whose emoji was encoded**: 55 documents, Emoji 13.1
through 17.0. Identified from Unicode's [emoji proposals
chart](https://unicode.org/emoji/charts/emoji-proposals.html), cross-referenced to the L2 registers,
downloaded and read on 2026-07-09. Texts are archived in
[`../proposals/reference-winners-2020-2024/`](../proposals/reference-winners-2020-2024/).

**Method caveat.** Statistics below count the PDF text layer. Most evidence in these proposals is
screenshots, and a screenshot of a Google Books Ngram chart contains no searchable text. One winner,
`L2/20-214` (X-ray), is a fully scanned PDF with zero extractable text. So counts of "how many cite Ngram"
understate reality and should not be quoted as compliance rates. Structure, length and licence lines are
measured reliably.

## The correction that matters first

I previously argued that an organization must carry the proposal, because every medical emoji in the
standard was filed by Emojination or its people. That evidence is real, and the conclusion was too strong.

Among the 55 winners:

- `L2/24-249` Orca, **Marcos Del Sol Vives**, one individual with a personal email address.
- `L2/24-255` Treasure Chest, **Jacob Dow**, alone.
- `L2/24-251` Bigfoot, **Andrew Pignato**, alone.
- `L2/23-255` Leafless Tree, **Brian Baihaki**, alone.
- `L2/20-214` **X-Ray**, Alijan Ozkiral and Christian Krenek. A **medical** emoji, filed by two
  individuals, in the very window our fifteen drafts were written, and it shipped in Emoji 14.0.

Individuals win regularly. Christian Krenek alone authored or co-authored the slide, the bird's nest, the
heavy equals sign, the flute and the x-ray. **What separates winners is craft, not letterhead.** An
organization helps because it teaches the craft and knows the route. It is not a substitute for the craft.

## The shape of a winner

`L2/24-249` Orca is the cleanest recent exemplar. Nine pages. Roughly 780 words of prose. Everything else
is screenshots. Its skeleton, verbatim:

```
1. IDENTIFICATION
     CLDR short name: orca
     CLDR keywords: killer whale
2. IMAGES
     18x18 / 72x72 / BW / Color
     License: The Nature Box (GFDL/CC-BY-SA)
3. SORT LOCATION
     Category: animal-marine   Location: after WHALE
4. REFERENCE EMOJI
     ELEPHANT
5. SELECTION FACTORS FOR INCLUSION
     Compatibility / Expected usage level / Multiple usages / Use in sequences /
     Breaking new ground / Image distinctiveness / Completeness: N/A /
     Frequently requested: N/A
6. SELECTION FACTORS FOR EXCLUSION
     Overly specific / Open ended / ...
```

Match that skeleton to today's required format. Then read the seven moves below, because the skeleton is
not what wins.

## The numbers

| Measure | Winners (n=55) | Our 2020 drafts (n=15) |
|---|---|---|
| Median words of prose | **907** | **1,467** |
| Longest | 2,758 | 1,759 |
| Over 3,000 words | 0 | 0 |
| Answers the Open-ended factor | 52 of 55 | 15 of 15 |
| States an image licence or credit | 46 of 55 | not stated in the drafts |

Winners are roughly forty percent shorter. Length is not rigour. The evidence is in the screenshots; the
prose exists to interpret it.

## The seven moves

### 1. Answer Unicode's factors, in Unicode's order, and write N/A without shame

Orca's answers to two inclusion factors, in full:

> **Completeness:** N/A.
> **Frequently requested:** N/A.

The guidelines say to do exactly this: *"Mark this as n/a unless there are compelling examples."* Our kidney
draft instead filled `Frequently Requested` with *"Kidneys are frequently referred to on the Internet,
including in memes."* That is now disallowed evidence, offered unprompted, in a slot that should have said
two letters.

### 2. Admit the weakness, then find where you win

Orca does not pretend to beat the elephant:

> "Results on YouTube show the same trend as in the rest of Google, with **orca being less popular than
> elephant**."
> "Google Trends shows that the popularity worldwide of the orca is **less than that of the elephant**."

Then:

> "However, the interest seems to be roughly the same for both animals **in the Hispanic community** (to
> which the author of this document belongs), specially for South America for which orca sometimes
> surpasses the popularity of the elephant."

This is the guideline *"Choose your language,"* executed. The proposal loses the global comparison, says so
plainly, and wins a regional one. A reviewer who catches you hiding a weakness stops believing your
strengths.

### 3. Use a Trends category, not a search term

> "Please note that although it says 'killer whale' instead of 'orca', it has been selected as **'animal'
> instead of 'search term'** to get correct results regardless of naming or language."

The guidelines say *"For Google Trends, please also use a qualifying category."* Almost nobody does it.
Orca does it, and explains why in one sentence. For a kidney the qualifying category is the anatomical
concept, not the string.

### 4. Cross-check with sources nobody asked for

Orca adds Bing, YouTube and **Wikipedia pageviews**:

> "Wikipedia shows that the popularity in term of pageviews is roughly the same for orcas and elephants,
> with many peaks for the former."

The five Google sources are mandatory. Extra reproducible sources are free credibility. Petitions, hashtags
and Instagram are not, and now count against you.

### 5. Put the image licence on the page

> Orca: `License: The Nature Box (GFDL/CC-BY-SA)`
> Lung, `L2/19-149`: *"Credit: Anna Zeng. Free for use in conjunction with this proposal"*

Forty-six of the 55 winners name a credit or a licence. Today, omitting it is an automatic rejection, and
the Emoji Proposal Agreement requires an irrevocable perpetual grant. Decide the artwork's provenance
before writing a word.

### 6. Keywords are not the name

| | Keywords as filed | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Orca | `killer whale` | encoded |
| Lung | `breath, inhalation, exhalation, respiration` | encoded |
| Heart | `heartbeat, pulse, center` | encoded |
| **Kidney** | **`Kidney`** | **declined** |

*"Do not repeat the name of the emoji. Consider terms that people would use to find this emoji."* And the
winners' keywords were adopted verbatim: `L2/19-190R` records `heartbeat | pulse | center | organ`.

### 7. Say what you will not come back for

The Open-ended factor asks *"If this emoji is added, will it result in the need to add other similar
types?"* Fifty-two of 55 winners answer it head on. Apple's accessibility set, `L2/18-080`, is the model:

> "we don't expect such discussion to lead to proposals for a large number of additions beyond the current
> proposal."

Our kidney draft had to argue this while thirteen sibling organ and device proposals sat beside it, proving
the opposite. **Filing fourteen medical concepts at once is itself the answer to the Open-ended question,
and the answer is yes.**

## What this means for the kidney

Draft the Open-ended answer first. If it cannot be written honestly, the proposal is not ready.

A defensible version: the kidney is the organ of the most common chronic disease conversation and of
transplantation. The proposal covers it alone. We do not intend to propose the pancreas, the gallbladder or
the spleen, and we say so here.

That sentence forbids filing the kidney alongside the liver and the stomach. Choose.

## Checklist

- [ ] Open-ended answer drafted first, naming what we will not return for
- [ ] Under 1,200 words of prose. Evidence carried by screenshots
- [ ] Sections in Unicode's order. `N/A` wherever honest
- [ ] `Frequently Requested` and every Instagram or petition citation deleted
- [ ] Keywords contain no form of the emoji's name
- [ ] All five mandated sources, each a screenshot, with `elephant` in Trends and Ngram
- [ ] Trends run on a qualifying category, and the choice explained in one sentence
- [ ] Searches hyphenated, in a private window, capture date stated
- [ ] A weakness identified and conceded, then reframed regionally or by language
- [ ] Image licence or credit named on the page, artwork textless, 18x18 and 72x72, colour and black and white
- [ ] `Reference Emoji` and `Sort Location` stated
- [ ] One emoji per proposal, unless the working group says otherwise
