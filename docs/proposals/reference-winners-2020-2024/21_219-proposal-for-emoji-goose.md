Proposal for Emoji: GOOSE

To: Unicode Consortium
From: Teal Starsong
Date: October 27, 2019; revised October 28, 2019

I am requesting the addition of a GOOSE emoji to complete the current lineup of waterfowl in the
Anatidae family. Geese are one of the earliest domesticated species in human history, with
documented cases going back to the beginning of written history, over 4000 years ago. While
ducks and swans are represented in emoji, the other major member of the same family is not.

Identification.

A. Suggested short name: GOOSE

1. Multiple usages:
    The "Golden Goose" is a well-known fairytale theme that entered common language
    use. "Mother Goose" is a common figure in nursery rhymes. Properties of geese
    such as being very vocal or very protective are also referenced in various languages.

2. Use in sequences:
    To show the variety of goose breeds, the proposed GOOSE emoji can be combined
    with ZWJ + skin tone markers or color markers, e.g. to symbolize grey geese, wild
    geese, etc.
    To symbolize the alertness and protectiveness, GOOSE could be joined by ZWJ to
    SPEAKER WITH HIGH VOLUME with a visual representation of a goose's vocalization
    ("honk").

3. Breaking new ground:
    Yes, as Geese are distinct from other species in the same family, such as ducks and
    swans, and have an important place in human history.

    C. Distinctiveness:
         Neither DUCK nor SWAN resemble ducks very much, both in body shape and
         plumage/coloring. Specifically, domestic geese are distinctive from most related waterfowl
         by their significantly different body posture with a fat behind and elongated neck.

    D. Completeness:
         GOOSE fills the gap between DUCK and SWAN and completes the Anatidae family.

Selection factors -- Exclusion.

E. Petitions or "frequent requests":
    No significant evidence, a few small forum threads and "one-off" petitions without much
    support.

F. Overly specific:
    No; Geese have many varieties. The domestic goose and white goose would be directly

with an extended neck. Combining variants could be used to represent more variations of geese
with different postures and colors.
