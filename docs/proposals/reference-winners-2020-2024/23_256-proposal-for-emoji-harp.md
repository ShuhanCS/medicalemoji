Abstract

This proposal requests the addition of the HARP emoji, representing a family of instruments
which has existed since antiquity in Asia, Africa and Europe, dating back at least as early as
3500 BC.
Introduction

                                   A harp found on Craigslist in Monterey, CA
Allow me to provide some essential background information from Wikipedia: "The harp is a
stringed musical instrument that has a number of individual strings running at an angle to its
soundboard; the strings are plucked with the fingers.... The instrument had great popularity in
Europe during the Middle Ages and Renaissance, where it evolved into a wide range of variants
with new technologies, and was disseminated to Europe's colonies, finding particular popularity
in Latin America.... Descendants of early harps are still played in Myanmar and parts of Africa,
and other defunct variants in Europe and Asia have been utilized by musicians in the modern
era....In terms of size, many smaller harps can be played on the lap, whereas larger harps are
quite heavy and rest on the floor. Different harps may use strings of catgut, nylon, metal, or
some combination."
Furthermore, angels are often depicted with harps, and we should all aspire to be angels.

Multiple Usages

HARP emoji would be used primarily to represent the instrument, but also to represent anything
angelic or Irish, which uses an image of a harp for an national emblem. According to Harp Wiki,
mythical figures such as Apollo and Orpheus are closely aligned with the harp.

Breaking New Ground

This proposal does not substantially break new ground though ancient instruments are not well
represented by the current emoji options.

Image Distinctiveness

Instruments such as the guitar and violin are somewhat related in their use of strings and
soundboards, but they're certainly not in the same family of instruments as the HARP and
LYRE, which are visually distincts.
Completeness

The harp is an egregious omission to the musical instruments represented. The harp is one of
the most recognizable instruments worldwide.

Frequently Requested
Selection Factors Exclusion

Overly Specific

The harp is a broad, fundamental family of instruments. A single glyph can represent a lyre,
cithara, clarsach, psaltery, zither or a traditional harp.

Open Ended

The harp emoji would aptly represent all of the harp variants.

Already Representable

As stated in the requests cited from Twitter, people are generally not satisfied using a guitar or
violin emoji for a harp, they are simply too different.

Transient

The harp will live on with humanity for as long as humanity exists! From thousands of years
BCE until today, the form and function of the harp has essentially remained the same.

Logos, brands, UI icons, signage, specific people, deities

The harp cannot be individual intellectual property. Countless businesses including
pharmaceutical companies, brands with Irish roots (real or feigned), and classical music
establishments incorporate a harp into their design mark, but the image is not specific to any
one brand or business. The harp alone does not represent any specific person, deity, logo or
brand.
