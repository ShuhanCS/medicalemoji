## Everyday / Non-Medical Uses

The Stomach emoji should not be framed only as a gastroenterology or disease symbol. It has broad everyday uses:

- Hunger and appetite.
- Fullness after eating.
- Digestion and meals.
- Upset stomach.
- Nausea.
- Stomach ache.
- Butterflies in the stomach.
- Gut feeling or intuition.
- "I can't stomach it" / tolerance or disgust.
- Belly/body-part communication.
- Food, nutrition, and eating contexts.

## Source Notes

### GERD / GORD

Scientific Reports published a global systematic review and meta-analysis estimating global pooled prevalence of gastro-oesophageal reflux disease at 13.98%, with an estimated 1.03 billion affected individuals.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-62795-1

### Functional Dyspepsia

Scientific Reports published a 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of functional dyspepsia according to Rome criteria, covering 44 studies, 256,915 participants, and 40 countries. The estimated global prevalence was 8.4%.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54716-3

### Stomach Cancer

WHO/IARC reported that, in 2022, stomach cancer accounted for about 970,000 new cases globally and about 660,000 deaths.

Source:
https://www.who.int/news/item/01-02-2024-global-cancer-burden-growing--amidst-mounting-need-for-services
