## 1. Identification

Suggested name: Stomach

Suggested keywords: digestion; hunger; appetite; nausea; stomach ache; stomach pain; upset stomach; gut; belly; abdomen; reflux; heartburn; indigestion; dyspepsia; gastroenterology; stomach cancer

Suggested category: People & Body, body-parts.

## 2. Images

The proposed image should represent the stomach as a recognizable organ used for digestion. It should remain legible at 18x18 pixels and should avoid text labels, logos, excessive anatomical detail, or a vendor-specific exact image request.

The main visual risk is ambiguity with generic organ shapes or food. The final packet should include a side-by-side visual review against existing organ emoji and common food/digestion emoji.

## 3. Factors For Inclusion

### A. Multiple Concepts

The Stomach emoji represents a broad and familiar concept, not a single disease, campaign, or medical specialty. It can be used for digestion, hunger, appetite, fullness, nausea, stomach ache, upset stomach, belly pain, reflux, heartburn, indigestion, dyspepsia, endoscopy, gastroenterology care, nutrition, meals, and stomach cancer.

The concept also has everyday non-medical meanings. People talk about a nervous "stomach," "butterflies in the stomach," a "gut feeling," having the stomach for something, being unable to stomach something, eating on an empty stomach, and feeling full or nauseated after a meal. These are durable phrases used across ordinary communication, not specialist jargon.

Medical and public-health evidence supports the durability of the concept. A Scientific Reports systematic review estimated global pooled prevalence of gastro-oesophageal reflux disease at 13.98%, corresponding to about 1.03 billion affected individuals:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-62795-1

A 2024 Scientific Reports systematic review estimated global prevalence of functional dyspepsia at 8.4% across Rome criteria:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54716-3

WHO/IARC reported about 970,000 new stomach cancer cases and about 660,000 stomach cancer deaths globally in 2022:
https://www.who.int/news/item/01-02-2024-global-cancer-burden-growing--amidst-mounting-need-for-services

These health sources are not substitutes for Unicode's required frequency evidence. They explain why the stomach is a durable, widely understood body concept with everyday, educational, clinical, nutritional, and metaphorical uses.

### B. Use In Sequences

Stomach can combine naturally with existing emoji:

- Stomach plus fork and knife: digestion, meals, appetite, or fullness.
- Stomach plus water drop: hydration, nausea, vomiting risk, or body function.
- Stomach plus face with thermometer or nauseated face: stomach flu, nausea, or feeling sick.
- Stomach plus warning sign: stomach pain, urgent symptoms, or food reaction.
- Stomach plus pill: antacid, medication effects, or treatment.
- Stomach plus hospital: gastroenterology visit, endoscopy, or clinical care.
- Stomach plus butterfly: butterflies in the stomach or nervous anticipation.
- Stomach plus brain or thought bubble: gut feeling or intuition.

These are ordinary emoji-composition examples. This proposal does not request any standardized sequence.

### C. Breaks New Ground

Stomach would add a distinct digestive organ concept that is not currently represented by existing emoji. Existing emoji can imply food, illness, or general medical care, but none directly represents the stomach as an organ for digestion, hunger, fullness, nausea, reflux, indigestion, stomach pain, or stomach cancer.

The proposal should not rely on the existence of anatomical heart, lungs, or brain as the main argument. Stomach should stand on its own broad usage, multiple concepts, visual recognizability, and inability to be represented clearly by existing emoji.

### D. Distinctiveness

The stomach has a recognizable curved organ shape in medical education and public illustrations. The final image should use a simple organ silhouette that remains recognizable at 18x18 pixels. The design should be tested against existing organ emoji, food emoji, and generic blob-like shapes.

### F. Completeness

Stomach would help fill a practical gap for common body, digestion, illness, eating, and gastroenterology communication. This is not a request to encode every organ. The case should be that stomach has independent broad usage, ordinary-language reach, and visual distinctiveness.

### G. Compatibility

No compatibility claim is currently made. If a documented high-usage stomach pictograph exists in a legacy carrier set or major platform, this section can be updated.

## 4. Factors For Exclusion

### A. Already Represented

Stomach is not already represented by existing emoji. Food emoji, nauseated face, hospital, pill, syringe, anatomical heart, lungs, and brain can support nearby meanings, but none identifies the stomach itself.

### B. Overly Specific

Stomach is not overly specific. It is a primary body part and digestive organ with broad everyday, educational, metaphorical, nutritional, symptom, and clinical uses.

### C. Open-Ended

The proposal should not be framed as part of an open-ended request for every organ. Stomach should be judged on its own evidence: high-frequency ordinary word, multiple concepts, visual recognizability, and broad use across everyday communication and health contexts.

### D. Transient

Stomach is not transient. Digestion, hunger, fullness, nausea, stomach ache, reflux, indigestion, stomach cancer, and metaphorical stomach/gut expressions are durable communication topics.

### E. Justified By An Existing Emoji

The proposal is not justified only by comparison to existing anatomical emoji. Existing anatomical emoji may show that body-part concepts can work as emoji, but Stomach must stand on independent usage and distinct meaning.
