## Seat 3 - Editorial/community reviewer

Verdict: `REVISE AND RERUN`

- One-sentence case: Liver lets people name the organ directly in messages about test results, medicine effects,
  transplantation and donation, anatomy, and food where generic medical or food emoji cannot do so.
- Strongest decline rationale: page 9's Open-ended formula—term frequency, several contexts, and a drawable
  silhouette—could be reused for many body parts, while some proposed sequences require explanatory prose.
- Source-discipline concern: page 3 lists education and food, but its institutional citations cover disease,
  tests, medicine monitoring, transplantation, and donation. Page 7 should say worldwide search interest, not
  worldwide use. “Recurring messages” is not directly measured.
- Editorial concern: `Liver + Hospital + People` is not self-explanatory; `medicine-related liver monitoring`,
  `body-site word`, and `carry the organ name` are less natural than direct message examples.
- Keep: the immediate `Yes` for Breaks new ground, the direct substitute analysis, and the concise N/A answers.
- Evidence: PDF pp.2-3 and p.9.

The editorial request to name other unencoded organs in the public Open-ended answer is rejected. It would pull
separate proposals into the Liver document and conflict with the stronger selection-seat advice. The accepted
goal is a sharper Liver-specific limiting principle without naming another unencoded proposal.
