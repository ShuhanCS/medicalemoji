## 1. Identification

Suggested name: Liver

Suggested keywords: liver; hepatic; organ; anatomy; body; digestion; metabolism; detox; cirrhosis; hepatitis; fatty liver; NAFLD; MASLD; liver cancer; transplant; donation; jaundice; alcohol

Suggested category: People & Body, body-parts.

## 2. Images

The proposed image should represent the liver as a recognizable organ. It should remain legible at 18x18 pixels and should avoid text labels, logos, excessive anatomical detail, or a vendor-specific exact image request.

The main visual risk is ambiguity with generic organ shapes or with raw meat/food. The final packet should include a side-by-side visual review against existing organ emoji (anatomical heart, lungs, brain) and against common food emoji.

## 3. Factors For Inclusion

### A. Multiple Concepts

The Liver emoji represents a broad and familiar concept, not a single disease, campaign, or medical specialty. It can be used for anatomy and biology education, digestion and metabolism, detoxification, nutrition (including liver as food), alcohol and health conversations, hepatitis awareness, fatty liver disease, cirrhosis, liver cancer, organ donation and transplantation, and general body/health communication.

The concept also has durable everyday and food meanings: liver as a dish (liver and onions, pâté, foie gras), "detox" and wellness culture, and common health conversations about alcohol and the liver. These are ordinary uses, not specialist jargon.

Medical and public-health context supports the durability of the concept (provided here as background, not as Unicode frequency evidence):

- Liver disease accounts for approximately 2 million deaths per year worldwide and is among the leading causes of death globally (attributed to Asrani et al., *Journal of Hepatology*, 2019 — TODO: confirm and link the primary source).
- Non-alcoholic / metabolic-associated fatty liver disease (NAFLD/MASLD) affects roughly a quarter of adults worldwide (TODO: confirm and link a current meta-analysis, e.g., Younossi et al.).
- WHO/IARC global cancer statistics report liver cancer among the leading causes of cancer death worldwide (TODO: confirm and link the current WHO/IARC figure).

These health sources are not substitutes for Unicode's required frequency evidence. They explain why the liver is a durable, widely understood body concept with everyday, educational, nutritional, and clinical uses.

### B. Use In Sequences

Liver can combine naturally with existing emoji:

- Liver plus fork and knife: liver as food, nutrition, or a meal.
- Liver plus wine glass: alcohol and liver health.
- Liver plus pill: medication, hepatotoxicity, or treatment.
- Liver plus hospital: hepatology visit or clinical care.
- Liver plus water drop or leafy green: detox, hydration, or wellness messaging.
- Liver plus warning sign: liver damage or urgent symptoms.

These are ordinary emoji-composition examples. This proposal does not request any standardized sequence.

### C. Breaks New Ground

Liver would add a distinct metabolic organ concept that is not currently represented by existing emoji. Existing emoji can imply food, illness, or general medical care, but none directly represents the liver as an organ for metabolism, detoxification, digestion support, hepatitis, fatty liver, cirrhosis, or liver cancer.

The proposal should not rely on the existence of anatomical heart, lungs, or brain as the main argument. Liver should stand on its own broad usage, multiple concepts, visual recognizability, and inability to be represented clearly by existing emoji.

### D. Distinctiveness

The liver has a recognizable wedge/lobed organ shape in medical education and public illustrations. The final image should use a simple organ silhouette that remains recognizable at 18x18 pixels. The design should be tested against existing organ emoji, food/meat emoji, and generic blob-like shapes.

### F. Completeness

Liver would help fill a practical gap for common body, food, metabolism, illness, and hepatology communication. This is not a request to encode every organ. The case should be that liver has independent broad usage, ordinary-language reach, and visual distinctiveness.

### G. Compatibility

No compatibility claim is currently made. If a documented high-usage liver pictograph exists in a legacy carrier set or major platform, this section can be updated.

## 4. Factors For Exclusion

### A. Already Represented

Liver is not already represented by existing emoji. Food emoji (including meat), hospital, pill, syringe, anatomical heart, lungs, and brain can support nearby meanings, but none identifies the liver itself.

### B. Overly Specific

Liver is not overly specific. It is a primary body part and metabolic organ with broad everyday, educational, nutritional, and clinical uses.

### C. Open-Ended

The proposal should not be framed as part of an open-ended request for every organ. Liver should be judged on its own evidence: high-frequency ordinary word, multiple concepts, visual recognizability, and broad use across everyday communication and health contexts.

### D. Transient

Liver is not transient. Metabolism, detox, nutrition, alcohol-and-health, hepatitis, fatty liver, cirrhosis, liver cancer, transplantation, and liver-as-food are durable communication topics.

### E. Justified By An Existing Emoji

The proposal is not justified only by comparison to existing anatomical emoji. Existing anatomical emoji may show that body-part concepts can work as emoji, but Liver must stand on independent usage and distinct meaning.
