## 3. Factors For Inclusion

### 3.1 Multiple Concepts

Potential concepts to support with citations/screenshots:

- Anatomy and organ identity.
- Kidney disease and CKD.
- Kidney donation and transplant.
- Dialysis and kidney failure.
- Kidney stones and acute patient symptoms.
- Filtration, urine, hydration, and toxicology contexts.
- Diabetes and hypertension complications.

Evidence needed:

- Search/trends screenshots.
- Public health source citations.
- Examples of ordinary-language usage that are not merely requests for an emoji.

### 3.2 Use In Sequences

Potential sequences:

- Kidney + water drop: urine, hydration, kidney filtration.
- Kidney + hospital: kidney care.
- Kidney + syringe: kidney labs, biopsy, treatment.
- Kidney + person: kidney donor/patient.
- Kidney + pill: nephrotoxic medication, kidney treatment.
- Kidney + warning: kidney injury.
- Kidney + blue circle or ribbon: kidney awareness if needed, though avoid relying on awareness cause.

Evidence needed:

- Explain use cases without claiming standardized ZWJ sequences.

### 3.3 Breaks New Ground

Argument to develop:

- Existing anatomical emoji include brain, anatomical heart, and lungs, but no kidney.
- Kidney would add a missing high-utility vital organ with distinct medical, transplant, donation, dialysis, and public-health use cases.
- Avoid making the argument only as "heart/lungs exist, so kidney should too"; Unicode warns against faulty comparison.

### 3.4 Distinctiveness

Argument to develop:

- The kidney has a familiar bean-shaped anatomical silhouette, paired arrangement, and ureter orientation.
- The design must show enough anatomy to avoid confusion with beans or generic organs at 18x18.

Evidence needed:

- 18x18 render tests.
- Side-by-side comparison with beans and existing organ emoji.
- Small-size readability review by non-nephrology viewers.

### 3.6 Completeness

Use carefully.

Potential argument:

- Kidney helps complete a small practical set of major human internal-organ emoji used in medical and public-health communication.

Risk:

- Unicode does not accept open-ended scientific/taxonomic completion arguments. Do not imply Unicode must encode every organ.

### 3.7 Compatibility

Default position:

- Mark n/a unless there is real evidence that a kidney pictograph is already high-frequency in a popular existing system.

## 4. Factors For Exclusion

### 4.1 Already Represented

Counterargument:

- Existing emoji do not directly represent kidney anatomy, kidney disease, kidney transplant, dialysis, kidney stones, or kidney donation.
- Beans are visually and semantically inadequate for medical contexts.

### 4.2 Overly Specific

Counterargument:

- Kidney is not a subtype of another encoded organ; it is a primary vital organ with broad clinical and everyday usage.

### 4.3 Open-Ended

Counterargument:

- The proposal is not a request to encode all organs. Kidney has independent expected usage because of CKD, dialysis, transplant, donation, stones, and its global public-health burden.

### 4.4 Transient

Counterargument:

- Kidney disease, donation, transplant, dialysis, and kidney stones are persistent topics, not fads.

### 4.5 Faulty Comparison

Counterargument:

- The proposal should not depend on the existence of anatomical heart or lungs. Those can be mentioned as context only.
