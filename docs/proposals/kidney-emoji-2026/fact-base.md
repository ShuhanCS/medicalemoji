# Kidney Emoji Fact Base

Last checked: 2026-05-13

This file separates citable facts from campaign rhetoric. For the proposal PDF, use the current sources below and cite screenshots or stable URLs for every factual claim.

## Current Global Burden

### WHO Kidney Disease Fact Sheet

Source:
https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/kidney-disease

Publication date on page: 2026-04-20.

Usable facts:

- Kidney disease includes acute kidney injury and chronic kidney disease.
- Simple blood serum creatinine and urine albuminuria tests used in primary care can detect CKD.
- WHO estimates that 674 million people have CKD worldwide.
- The most severe form of CKD is kidney failure, which requires dialysis or kidney transplantation to sustain life.
- Kidney disease is often asymptomatic until late stages.
- The burden of kidney disease is rising in parallel with diabetes, hypertension, and population ageing.
- Global inequities in testing, medications, health-care workers, and kidney replacement therapy contribute to preventable deaths.
- In May 2025, the World Health Assembly adopted a kidney-health resolution calling for early detection, management, and equitable access.

Proposal use:

- Use this as the conservative, official global-health source.
- Avoid mixing WHO's 674 million CKD estimate with older 850 million "some form of kidney disease" campaign language unless the distinction is explicit.

### Lancet / GBD 2023 Chronic Kidney Disease Collaborators

Source:
https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/global-regional-and-national-burden-of-chronic-kidney-disease-in-/

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)01853-7

Publication: Lancet, 2025; 406(10518):2461-2482.

Usable facts:

- In 2023, 788 million adults aged 20 years and older were estimated to have CKD.
- Global age-standardized adult CKD prevalence was 14.2%.
- In 2023, CKD was the ninth leading cause of death globally.
- CKD accounted for 1.48 million deaths globally in 2023.
- CKD was the 12th leading cause of DALYs.
- Impaired kidney function as a risk factor accounted for 11.5% of cardiovascular deaths.
- Leading CKD DALY risk factors included high fasting plasma glucose, high body-mass index, and systolic blood pressure.

Proposal use:

- Use this for the strongest peer-reviewed adult burden statement.
- State that this is adults age 20+ and is not the same denominator as WHO's 674 million global CKD estimate.

### WHO Top Causes Of Death

Source:
https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/the-top-10-causes-of-death

Usable facts:

- WHO's Global Health Estimates state that kidney diseases rose from the world's 19th leading cause of death to ninth.
- WHO reports kidney disease deaths increased by 95% between 2000 and 2021.

Proposal use:

- Use as a global salience fact, not as frequency evidence.

## Current United States Burden

### CDC Chronic Kidney Disease In The United States

Source:
https://www.cdc.gov/kidney-disease/php/data-research/index.html

Usable facts:

- More than 1 in 10 US adults aged 18 or older, 14%, were estimated to have CKD.
- This corresponds to 37 million people.
- About 9 in 10 adults aged 20 or older with CKD did not know they have CKD.
- CKD prevalence was higher among adults with diabetes, high blood pressure, and older age.
- CKD was more common in non-Hispanic Black adults than in non-Hispanic White or Hispanic adults.

Proposal use:

- Use to support public-health communication and detection relevance in the United States.
- Do not let the US evidence dominate; Unicode requires worldwide expected usage.

### CDC CKD: Common, Serious, And Costly

Source:
https://www.cdc.gov/kidney-disease/ckd-facts/index.html

Usable facts:

- More than 1 in 7 US adults may have CKD.
- As many as 9 in 10 do not know they have it.
- Kidney diseases are a leading cause of death in the United States.
- About 360 people begin treatment for kidney failure every day.

## Claims To Avoid Or Reframe

- Avoid saying "850 million people have CKD" unless the exact source says CKD. Older materials often use "kidney diseases" or "some form of kidney disease," which is broader.
- Avoid relying on petitions, signatures, hashtags, or society letters as proof of expected usage.
- Avoid saying kidney should be approved because heart and lungs were approved. Unicode explicitly flags faulty comparison as an exclusion factor.
- Avoid claiming the emoji would directly improve quality of care unless supported by a specific study.
- Avoid a proposal that opens the door to every organ; the proposal must explain why kidney is broad, iconic, and high-utility.

## Strongest Fact Pattern For Proposal Drafting

Kidney is a globally recognizable paired organ with high public-health salience. Current evidence supports global and US disease burden, rising mortality, large undiagnosed populations, dialysis/transplant relevance, and links to diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, donation, and transplant communication. These facts support public-health relevance, but the acceptance case still depends on current Unicode frequency evidence and visual distinctiveness.
