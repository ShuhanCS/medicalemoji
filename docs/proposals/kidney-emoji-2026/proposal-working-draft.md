## 3. Factors For Inclusion

### A. Multiple Usages

The kidney emoji can represent several common concepts without being limited to one disease or one medical specialty:

- Human anatomy and organ identity.
- Kidney function, filtration, urine production, hydration, and electrolyte balance.
- Chronic kidney disease and kidney failure.
- Dialysis and kidney replacement therapy.
- Kidney transplant and living/deceased donor communication.
- Kidney stones and acute kidney pain.
- Acute kidney injury and medication safety.
- Diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular risk communication where kidney complications are central.
- Public-health campaigns around early detection and kidney health.

Support to include:

- Current WHO kidney disease fact sheet.
- CDC CKD data for United States context.
- Peer-reviewed global burden evidence.
- Objective search/trends/ngram screenshots.

### B. Use In Sequences

Possible non-standard sequences:

- Kidney + water drop: hydration, urine, filtration.
- Kidney + hospital: kidney care or nephrology appointment.
- Kidney + pill: medication safety or kidney treatment.
- Kidney + syringe: labs, biopsy, treatment, or transplant-related care.
- Kidney + warning: kidney injury or urgent symptoms.
- Kidney + person: donor, recipient, patient, clinician conversation.
- Kidney + blue circle/ribbon: kidney awareness, if used carefully as a secondary context.

These are examples of ordinary emoji composition, not proposed ZWJ sequences.

### C. Breaks New Ground

Kidney would add a missing high-utility internal organ with distinct public-health, transplant, donation, dialysis, stone, and medication-safety uses. It is not merely another anatomy item. Kidney-specific communication is common across patient care, patient advocacy, transplant/donation messaging, nephrology education, and everyday health conversations.

Existing anatomical emoji can be mentioned as context, but the proposal should stand on kidney's independent expected usage and distinctive meaning.

### D. Distinctiveness

Kidney has a recognizable bean-shaped anatomical silhouette, but that creates a risk of confusion with bean emoji or food. The final design needs small-size testing to show that the emoji remains a kidney at 18x18.

Evidence to add:

- 18x18 and 72x72 render tests.
- Side-by-side comparisons with beans and existing organ emoji.
- Quick readability review from non-nephrology viewers.

### F. Completeness

Use this argument narrowly. Kidney helps complete a practical set of common internal-organ concepts used in medical and public-health communication. Do not frame this as a request to encode every organ.

### G. Compatibility

Default position: not applicable unless we find strong evidence that a kidney pictograph is already used in a popular existing platform or communication system.

## 4. Factors For Exclusion

### A. Already Representable

No existing emoji directly represents kidney anatomy, kidney disease, kidney transplant, dialysis, living donation, kidney stones, or kidney-specific medical communication. Beans are visually and semantically inadequate, especially for clinical or patient-facing use.

### B. Overly Specific

Kidney is a primary human organ and a broad health concept. It is not a subtype of another organ and is not limited to one condition, one procedure, or one profession.

### C. Open-Ended

The proposal should explicitly state that kidney has independent usage grounds. The case is based on kidney disease burden, transplant/donation communication, dialysis, stones, AKI, medication safety, hydration/urine/filtration, and public-health communication. It is not a request for a complete anatomy taxonomy.

### D. Transient

Kidney health, kidney disease, transplant, donation, dialysis, and kidney stones are enduring topics. The emoji would not be tied to a temporary event or campaign.

### E. Faulty Comparison

The proposal should not argue that kidney deserves encoding simply because heart, brain, or lungs exist. Those emoji may be context, but the kidney proposal must stand on objective expected usage and distinct meaning.
