## What The Old Proposal Did Well

- It framed the kidney as a core human organ, not merely a disease-awareness symbol.
- It included several everyday and clinical meanings: urination, kidney stones, UTI complications, transplant, donation, kidney disease, dialysis-adjacent communication, food/kidney beans, and the kidney dish.
- It recognized the need to address Unicode exclusion factors: overly specific, open-ended, already representable, transient, faulty comparison, and image specificity.
- It included a broad supporter list from nephrology societies and patient organizations.

## Main Problems To Fix For A Renewed Submission

1. The old proposal relied too heavily on medical importance and supporter credibility.

Current Unicode review is stricter about objective expected-usage evidence. The renewed proposal needs reproducible screenshots and counts from Google Search, Google Video Search, Google Trends Web Search, Google Trends Image Search, and Google Books Ngram, with the required comparator terms.

2. Several factual claims need refresh or replacement.

The old draft used older disease-burden, transplant-waitlist, kidney-stone, and UTI statistics. The renewed proposal should cite current WHO, CDC, and peer-reviewed sources, and should avoid using older numbers unless they are still the best available source and date-labeled.

3. The "breaking new ground" section was weak.

The old draft said "No. The BRAIN emoji has already opened the door for anatomical organs." The renewed version should avoid a pure comparison to existing anatomical emoji. It should argue that kidney independently adds a high-utility, visually distinct, globally recognizable medical/public-health concept that is not covered by existing emoji.

4. Image distinctiveness was underdeveloped.

The old draft said the kidney is distinct, but did not prove legibility at 18x18 or explain how to avoid confusion with beans. The renewed version needs 18x18 and 72x72 color/black-and-white images, plus side-by-side visual tests against beans and existing anatomy emoji.

5. The proposal used phrases that could trigger open-ended-organ concerns.

The renewed version should state clearly that this is not a request for every organ. Kidney has independent expected usage because of kidney disease, dialysis, transplant, living donation, kidney stones, AKI, medication safety, hydration/urine/filtration, diabetes, hypertension, and public-health communication.

6. The old proposal's image and license chain are not submission-ready.

Unicode needs a clear rights statement for proposal images. The recovered old WordPress kidney art is useful historical material, but the renewed submission should either use original assigned artwork or artwork with a documented compatible open license.

## Reusable Arguments

- Kidney has multiple meanings across anatomy, physiology, kidney disease, transplant, donation, dialysis, stones, medication safety, hydration, urine, and public health.
- Existing emoji do not adequately represent kidney anatomy or kidney-specific care.
- Beans are not acceptable substitutes in clinical or public-health communication.
- Kidney is durable and not a transient campaign symbol.
- The coalition support is broad across patients, clinicians, nephrology societies, and international groups.

## Arguments To Avoid Or Reframe

- Avoid "brain/heart/lungs exist, therefore kidney should exist" as the main case.
- Avoid treating the old support letters as frequency evidence.
- Avoid broad "all organs should be represented" completeness logic.
- Avoid old unsupported claims about direct care improvement.
- Avoid claims based on petitions, social media asks, or "people want this emoji" unless used only as coalition context.
