# Abstract

We are requesting the addition of a PILL PACK (OBJECT) emoji. Pill packs
portray medication in a recognizable way to many consumers and patients.
Pill packs also assist patients taking medication by using visual cues
to help a patient track their medication intake. Ensuring patient
adherence to recommendations and prescriptions is a significant factor
in improving health outcomes.

Additionally, the PILL PACK offers medical professionals a digital tool
in the fight against ongoing opioid addiction and the opioid epidemic.
The Pill Pack emoji closely resembles Suboxone, which can be prescribed
by doctors and is a proven and preferred treatment against opioid
addiction.

Assisting medical professionals in conveying common and helpful medical
objects like pill packs will promote public health. Furthermore, as
tele-health usage increases, the emojification of a highly common object
like the pill pack will improve digital communication between medical
professionals and patients.

**Introduction**

Invented in the 1960s, pill packs, often called blister packs, grew in
popularity after birth control pills were often packaged this way. The
typical pill pack features a breakable foil seal paired with a plastic
container that houses the medicine. This low cost design, along with
other factors, caused the pill pack to grow in popularity in the medical
community. Currently, 85% of solid pills in Europe and 20% in the United
States are packaged in pill packs.

Along with the low cost design, an advantage of the pill pack versus
other pill containers is the ease at which patients can track their pill
intake. This is relevant for the significant number of patients who
suffer from memory loss (such as dementia patients) and those who are
prescribed multiple daily pills. Patient medication practices often have
a wide disparity between dosage prescribed by medical experts and the
amount taken by a patient, otherwise called adherence, resulting in poor
health outcomes. One study found 76% of patients did not accurately
follow prescribed dosage, with likelihood of not following prescribed
amounts increasing with patient age and number of pills needed. A
difference in prescribed daily pill intake from one to three was shown
to decrease adherence from 84% to 59%. Patients with thirteen or more
daily pills had an adherence rate as low as 20%.

Pill packs range in complexity, but all follow a standard design. With
many high-risk patients, such as HIV/AIDS patients, diabetics, or those
suffering from heart disease, relying on a consistent pill regimen,
access to low-cost reminders of their daily/periodic regimen is shown to
greatly improve health outcomes. Expanding the cues, especially digital
cues, patients receive to take their medication would likely further
improve adherence.

Lastly, the addition of a PILL PACK emoji serves an immediate
medium-term purpose. As medical professionals combat opioid epidemics
worldwide, the addition of an emoji that closely resembles pills highly
effective against opioid addiction and overdose would greatly help
transmit information to patients in times of crisis. Suboxone and
Naltrexone are both highly effective against opioid-abuse issues and are
often circular pills and/or packaged in blister packs that are depicted
in the PILL PACK emoji. An accessible symbol of these types of drugs
would be extremely helpful in combating opioid epidemics.

The addition of PILL PACK (OBJECT) would help medical professionals
communicate to many patients a low-cost and effective solution to a
serious problem in healthcare. The accessibility of pill packs improves
health outcomes and granting medical professionals a simple symbol for
the tool will allow for greater access to a simple solution.

## Image Distinctiveness

The PILL PACK (OBJECT) is distinct from other emojis, such as the
PACKAGE (OBJECT), as it is easily identifiable as a package for
dispensing medicine. Pill packs are simple and easily identifiable at
most scales.

## Completeness

The PILL PACK (OBJECT) does help in completeness. The PILL (OBJECT) is
the closest existing emoji to the purpose of the pill pack but differs
in some important ways. First, The pill pack emoji resembles medical
packaging very commonly prescribed by doctors and taken by patients.
Second, the pill pack emoji depicts compressed and circular pills, which
differs from the cylindrical shape of the PILL emoji, broadening the
descriptive tools medical professionals can use.

# Selection Factors – Exclusion

## Petitions or Frequently Requested

Not Applicable.

## Overly Specific

Pill packs are used by patients with a wide variety of ailments and are
not specific to a subgroup. Pills sold in packaging similar to the pill
pack emoji include some of the most commonly prescribed drugs by medical
professionals. Therefore the PILL PACK (OBJECT) emoji is not niche.

## Open-ended

The introduction of PILL PACK (OBJECT) emoji will not lead to the
introduction of an infinite set of emojis as the pill pack is one of the
only ways doctor-prescribed drugs are sold and taken.

Already Representable

The current crop of medical emojis is limited and does not represent the
diverse objects medical professionals and patients interact with. The
closest emoji to the PILL PACK (OBJECT) is the PILL (OBJECT). The
introduction of the pill pack represents a more diverse depiction of the
types of medicine patients and doctors frequently interact with.

Logos, brands, UI icons, signage, specific people, specific landmarks,
deities

There are no logos, brands, deities, specific people or landmarks
represented by this. Many pharmaceutical brands use a packaging
resembling this emoji, but the emoji design is not distinct as to appear
similar to specific brands.

## Transient

Pill packs have been used in the pharmaceutical industry for decades and
their low-cost design means the design is unlikely to dramatically
change in the next 50 years.

## Faulty Comparison

We are comparing it mostly to the PILL (OBJECT), which is an appropriate
benchmark emoji to compare it to, so it is not faulty. We believe the
PILL PACK (OBJECT) represents a pill distinct from the existing PILL
emoji as it represents a more compressed and circular pill also common
in pharmaceutical manufacturing. Common prescriptions such as
<span class="mark">Aripiprazole or Rosuvastatin appear in pharmacies in
containers resembling the PILL PACK emoji.</span>

## Exact Images

PILL PACKS (OBJECT) are recognizable by their shape and multiple
compartment features. Pill packs are used by many pharmaceutical
companies and thus vary in small ways. This allows for variation in
color and compartment number, while still being recognizable as a pill
pack. This proposal does not require a specific representation.
