# Introduction

Most people know that a major function of the intestines is for
digestion of the consumption of foods as well as excretion of the
processed foods. This fact alone renders the intestines a core part of
not only human health, but of our conscious lives. By some estimates,
people spend an average of 270 days completing a bowel movement over the
course of a lifetime assuming a global life expectancy of 70 years. In a
normal range, most people have anywhere from one bowel movement every
three days to three bowel movements per day. People may also have more
bowel movements if they are taking certain medications such as
laxatives, stool softeners, fiber supplements, etc.

The INTESTINES are a tube-like group of organs that extends from the
stomach to the anus. It is one of the most iconic group of organs in the
body and occupies a critical role in the digestive system. Though there
are a plethora of food emojis, none represent the intestines. It is also
a critical organ for producing substances that carry messages to other
parts of the body, immunological function for fighting germs, and
regulating the body’s water balance.

Intestines are also a critical piece of public and private dialogue
about human health. Chronic diarrhea and constipation are a couple of
the most common conditions people have. Additionally, an estimated 1.3%
of U.S. adults (3 million) had been diagnosed with Inflammatory Bowel
Disease (IBD) which could have been either crohn’s disease or ulcerative
colitis. This was a large increase from 1999 (0.9% or 2 million adults).
There is currently no definitive treatment but rather management and
surgery. Treatment may include immunosuppressive drugs that control the
inflammation. However, this often leaves patients in a susceptible state
to other common clinical conditions.

In addition, colorectal cancer accounts for over ~150,000 cases per
year. Deaths from colorectal cancer among people younger than age 55
have increased 1% per year from 2008 and 2017. In fact, a famous actor
from Black Panther, Chadwick Boseman, recently passed away from this.
Given the severity of the condition and its many various forms of
presentation, this can be extremely life-threatening and cause death if
left undiagnosed.
