# Abstract

This proposal requests the addition of a LEG CAST (OBJECT) emoji. The
LEG CAST (OBJECT) is made up of several components. The outside is made
up of plaster which is white in color (pictured) and fiberglass. Cotton
and other synthetic materials line the inside to provide soft padding
around the joint areas. This holds a broken bone involving the leg in
place as it heals. Casts aim to prevent and decrease muscle contractures
and immobilization, often after surgeries. Casts are meant to immobilize
the joint above and below the area that is to be kept straight and
without motion.

This specified cast is clinically named the short leg cast, which is
applied to the area below the knee to the foot. This cast is used for
lower leg fractures and severe ankle sprains/strains and fractures as
well as holding the leg and foot muscles and connective tissue in place
after surgery to facilitate healing.

This highlights an important opportunity to improve public health by
making available a leg cast emoji for digital communication.

# Introduction

The first method of casts was listed as “plaster-of-paris” evolving
around the mid 1700s. It was more an “accidental” discovery when
transporting injured military personnel to and from locations. Since
then, it has undergone several iterations and evolved into the modern
day cast for severe musculoskeletal injuries involving fractures,
sprains, and connective tissue injuries.

Casts are a critical piece of public and private dialogue about human
health. Around six-million people in the United States alone break a
bone each year. In fact, on average, every person will experience two
broken bones/fractures over the course of a lifetime. Specifically,
around 16% of all musculoskeletal injuries in the u.S. are due to
fractures annually. They occur at an annual rate of 2.4 per 100
population. Further, approximately 887,679 hospitalizations result each
year from fractures, with appxoaimtly 3.5 million visits made to
emergency departments for fractures each year. In 1998, the most
frequently resulting in hospitalization were hip fractures (323,522),
ankle fractures (101,944), and fractures of the tibia and fibula
(67,600). These are all lower extremity fractures.

With broken bones being so common, casts are critical and essential for
healing these injuries. They are orthopedic devices that are used to
protect and support injured and fractured bones and joints. They
immobilize the injured limb to stabilize the bone in place until it
fully heals. They are made from fiberglass or plaster.

Further, these casts improve clinical outcomes by allowing somebody who
otherwise would not have been able to walk properly be able to walk
again. Emotionally and psychologically, this can be a largely positive
and restorative experience. For somebody to be able to walk again, write
again, turn the back voluntarily, etc., are often taken for granted. But
if you walk into any rehabilitation hospital, you will immediately see
the emotional recovery and comfort that the full rehabilitation team
provides. Enabling them to perform their jobs is often a cast that
allows patients to undergo some type of therapy. The gift of restored
functionality and activities of daily living (ADLs) cannot be
understated. ADLs commonly involved (but are not limited to)
dressing/bathing, eating, ambulating/walking, toileting, hygiene. Casts
are often at the forefront and foundation to enable this type of healing
and restorative function.
