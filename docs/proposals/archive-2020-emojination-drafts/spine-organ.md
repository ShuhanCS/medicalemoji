# Abstract

This proposal requests the addition of a SPINE emoji. The vertebral
column is often colloquially called the SPINE because the bumps on the
back of the vertebrae are called spines. The SPINE is a column of
alternating spinal bones, called vertebra, and cushioning vertebral
discs. The SPINE is essential for structural support of the body and
protection of the spinal cord.

Low back pain is one of the most common medical complaints in the United
States and accounts for an estimated \$240 billion of healthcare costs
in the United States alone. Because of its important roles in supporting
structure and protective function, damage anywhere along the SPINE often
results in disabling pain, neurological issues (like loss of sensation),
and diminished range of motion. The potential for patients and providers
to communicate about the vertebral column in a simple and generic way
highlights an important opportunity to improve public health by making a
SPINE emoji available for digital communication.

# Introduction

a sore knot in their back. In fact, back pain is one of the most common

## Image Distinctiveness

The SPINE is distinct from the SKULL and BONE emoji because it
represents a very unique bony structure. The transverse processes (the
bumps on the sides) and the alternation between vertebrae and vertebral
discs make the image unique and yet generic enough to represent any
section of the vertebral column.

## Completeness

The spine is an important component to bring completeness to the Body
Parts category of emoji. With body parts like the brain, arm, and ear
represented and bony body parts like the skull and bone, adding SPINE
would give an opportunity to communicate more.

# Selection Factors – Exclusion

## Petitions or Frequently Requested

Not Applicable.

## Overly Specific

The SPINE is not too overly specific because it can represent any part
of the back while being distinct from generic BONE. The existing emoji
SKULL would be more specific than the SPINE because the SPINE could
represent any part of the spine.

## Open-ended

This is not open ended, because SPINE could represent any section of the
vertebral column (neck, back, or low back) or the vertebral column as a
whole. With the SKULL, BONE, and SPINE emojis, nearly every bone in the
body could be represented with an emoji.

## Already Representable

The spine has structure and function unique from the other bones of the
body, so BONE and SKULL would not be used to represent the vertebral
column.

## Logos, brands, UI icons, signage, specific people, specific landmarks, deities 

There are no logos, brands, deities, specific people or landmarks
represented by this.

## Transient

As long as humans have the biological form we are all familiar with, the
spine will be a subject of curiosity, medical practice, and
conversation. In addition, the increasing prevalence of spinal pain that
has been correlated to people being more and more sedentary and seated
will likely only grow the frequency of conversation related to the
SPINE.

## Faulty Comparison

While the SPINE would play a role similar to SKULL and BONE, it is
distinct in the body section that it represents, so no comparisons are
being made with any other emoji.

## Exact Images

One of the benefits of the SPINE emoji is that it can represent any area
of the spine and is not specific to any one part. As mentioned before,
the SPINE has generic and recognizable traits in the transverse
processes (the bumps on the sides) and the alternation between vertebrae
and vertebral discs. The SPINE emoji does not need to be a very detailed
image, but can be generic and versatile.
