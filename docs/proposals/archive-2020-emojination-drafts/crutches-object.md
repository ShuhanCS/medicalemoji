# Abstract

This proposal requests the addition of a CRUTCHES (OBJECT) emoji. The
CRUTCHES (OBJECT) is a medical device aimed to aid in ambulation by
transferring body weight from the legs to the torso and arms. Though
there are a plethora of physical exercises and some rehabilitation
emojis, none represent crutches, one of the most common medical
equipment used to assist in orthopedic repair and neurologic
impairments.

Worldwide, the number of crutch uses is growing and axilla crutches are
used the most. Crutches have been indicated for patients with compromise
of lower extremity balance and weight bearing and who also have
sufficient upper body strength and coordination to move their own body
weight. Further, there are several types of crutches, with axilla
crutches being the most common type (shown above). Most health care
facilities discharge patients with axilla crutches for immediate use.

This highlights an important opportunity to improve public health by
making available a crutches emoji for digital communication.

# Introduction

Around six-million people in the United States alone break a bone each
year. In fact, on average, every person will experience two broken
bones/fractures over the course of a lifetime. Specifically, around 16%
of all musculoskeletal injuries in the U.S. are due to fractures
annually. They occur at an annual rate of 2.4 per 100 population.
Further, approximately 887,679 hospitalizations result each year from
fractures, with appxoaimtly 3.5 million visits made to emergency
departments for fractures each year. In 1998, the most frequently
resulting in hospitalization were hip fractures (323,522), ankle
fractures (101,944), and fractures of the tibia and fibula (67,600).
These are all lower extremity fractures that can commonly require a
crutch to aid with healing. Further, over 6.8 million community-resident
Americans use assistive devices to help with mobility. Of this number,
6.1 million users use canes, crutches, and/or walkers.

With broken bones being so common, crutches are a critical piece of
public and private dialogue about human health and essential to heal all
the above injuries, They are used to offload body weight to the injured
extremity and thus provide optimal conditions to enable healing of acute
injuries. They further provide ambultatory support and mobility options
to those with neurologic injuries and chronic orthopedic injuries
enabling the individual to stay mobile where they otherwise may have not
been able to.

Further, crutches improve clinical outcomes by allowing somebody who
otherwise would not have been able to walk properly be able to.
Emotionally and psychologically, this can be a largely positive and
restorative experience. If you walk into any rehabilitation session,
crutches often allow patients to undergo some type of therapy and as
well. The gift of restored functionality and activities of daily living
(ADLs) cannot be understated. ADLs commonly involved (but are not
limited to) dressing/bathing, eating, ambulating/walking, toileting,
hygiene. Crutches are often at the forefront and foundation to enable
this type of daily routine that patients have rather than be handicapped
and otherwise need somebody to help move them.
