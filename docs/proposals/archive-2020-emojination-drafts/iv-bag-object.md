# Abstract

We are requesting the addition of an IV BAG (OBJECT) emoji. IV Bags,
short for intravenous bags, are used in medical settings to store
solution that is injected into patients via their veins. Often, the
solution is part Sodium Chloride, which restores water levels, and
liquid relevant to the patient’s condition. IV bags are used almost
anytime a patient is checked into a hospital, to help monitor water
levels and provide direct access to a vein if a medication needs to be
administered.

We believe the addition of an IV Bag emoji will be helpful in educating
the public on what they interact with so frequently upon hospital
visits. Furthermore, IV Bags can symbolize a myriad of situations
medical professionals face and help with communication between staff.

# Introduction

The use of IV treatment dates back to the 1830s, when doctors used a
similar method to combat cholera<span class="mark">. The treatment
became more common in the 1890s before becoming widespread in the 1950s.
The treatment, which often inserts a needle in an accessible vein,
slowly drips liquid solution into the person’s vein, providing immediate
treatment into the bloodstream.</span>

<span class="mark">Today, it is estimated over 300 million IV bags are
used in the United States per year. The primary purpose of IV bags is to
immediately stabilize a patient upon entry into a hospital. This
warrants the emojification of the object. There are multiple scenarios
in which the availability on an IV bag emoji would ease communication,
especially in high pressure scenarios like an emergency room. A patient
that is somewhat incapacitated or is hindered by a language barrier will
still understand the visual presented to them.</span>

<span class="mark">We believe the ubiquity of IV bags in hospital
settings warrant the emojification of the object. There can be multiple
scenarios in which a medical professional could symbolize a procedure, a
process, or a hospital area. The introduction of this symbol would
expand the tools medical professionals have to communicate with patients
and each other.</span>

## Image Distinctiveness

The IV BAG emoji is distinctly an IV BAG and not similar to other
existing emojis. It is somewhat similar to a BLOOD BAG emoji, but is
distinct in two important ways: it features a clear liquid versus a red
(blood) liquid, and it clearly displays it’s interior as a Sodium
Chloride solution. On an emoji scale, the clear bag and shape conjure up
an IV bag.

## Completeness

The existing options for medical emojis are limited in their coverage of
the type of equipment used in medical settings. Tools like the SYRINGE,
TEST TUBE, and others are common and the addition of the IV BAG would
help fill out the medical emoji category.

# Selection Factors – Exclusion

## Petitions or Frequently Requested

Not Applicable.

## Overly Specific

IV BAGS (OBJECT) deserve to be an emoji as they are very common within a
medical setting. It is estimated over 300 million IV bags are used every
year in the United States. On average, that means a little less than one
American gets treated with an IV Bag each year. We believe the broad use
of IV bags make them general use and not overly niche.

## Open-ended

The introduction of the IV BAG (OBJECT) would not lead to an infinite
set of new emojis. Few medical tools are used as frequently as IV bags.
Existing emojis reflect this finding. The STETHOSCOPE and SYRINGE are
associated with the hospitals and are frequently used by medical
professionals. The same is true of IV bags, they are used frequently to
stabilize patients and ensure hydration during otherwise stressful
times. Using this logic, an IV bag emoji is warranted and would not
allow for many more emojis.

## Already Representable

The most similar current emoji, the SYRINGE (OBJECT) does represent a
tool medical professionals use to inject patients with fluids, but
otherwise differs from the IV BAG (OBJECT). Whereas syringes are used by
medical professionals to administer flu shots or other one-time
injections, patients are hooked up to IV bags upon admission to ER or
when a patient is suffering from dehydration, pain, or in need of a
blood transfusion. No existing emoji represents the emergency room
setting, or a patient being treated for severe injuries, as an IV bag
does.

## Logos, brands, UI icons, signage, specific people, specific landmarks, deities 

There are no logos, brands, deities, specific people or landmarks
represented by this. Many companies create IV bags, but this emoji makes
no particular reference to a certain brand.

## Transient

IV BAGS (OBJECT) have resembled the proposed emoji for almost 100 years,
and for the next 50 years it is unlikely the IV bag will change so to
make this emoji outdated. The main advantage of the IV bag, their low
cost, will be unlikely to change. Furthermore, the injection solution,
sodium chloride, is always used in IV injections to restore water and
salt levels. Other drugs may be added depending on the patient, but the
sodium chloride solution will remain applicable in 50 years.

## Faulty Comparison

The only emoji we frequently compare the IV BAG (OBJECT) to is SYRINGE
(OBJECT), which we believe is a fair comparison since they are both used
by medical professionals to inject patients with fluids. Otherwise, we
believe we have found a gap in the emoji database that would benefit
from the addition of the IV BAG.

## Exact Images

IV BAGS (OBJECT) are standard medical tools and thus have some degree of
variation due their popularity. There are some characteristics common to
all IV bags, such as a stem to connect the solution to the patient.
Outside of these common IV bag features, there can be variability in
shape and size of the emoji.
