# Abstract

We are requesting the addition of a PILL BOX emoji. PILL BOXES (OBJECT)
assist patients taking medication by using visual and audio cues to
remind patients when to take prescribed doses. Ensuring patient
adherence to recommendations and prescriptions is a significant factor
in improving health outcomes. Simple and low cost cues help the widest
variety of patients in adhering to their prescriptions, meaning objects
like pill boxes are recognizable to a wide swath of the population. Pill
boxes have demonstrated success in improving an important aspect of
patient compliance: ensuring patients with daily multi-pill regimens
and/or memory problems accurately follow prescribed dosage.

Assisting medical professionals in conveying common and helpful medical
objects like pill boxes will increase public health. Furthermore, as
tele-health usage increases, the emojification of a highly common tool
like the pill box will improve digital communication between medical
professionals and patients.

**Introduction**

Pill boxes are used by patients with a variety of ailments, including
people suffering from memory loss such as dementia patients and those
prescribed multiple daily pills. Patient medication practices often have
a wide disparity between dosage prescribed by medical experts and the
amount taken by a patient, otherwise called adherence, resulting in poor
health outcomes. One study found 76% of patients did not accurately
follow prescribed dosage, with likelihood of not following prescribed
amounts increasing with patient age and number of pills needed. A
difference in prescribed daily pill intake from one to three was shown
to decrease adherence from 84% to 59%. Patients with thirteen or more
daily pills had an adherence rate as low as 20%.

Pill boxes range in complexity, but all follow a standard design. More
innovative pill boxes use sensors to relay pill removal to medical
professionals, further ensuring patients strictly follow their
prescribed dosages. With many high-risk patients, such as HIV/AIDS
patients, diabetics, or those suffering from heart disease, relying on
consistent dosage intake, access to low-cost reminders of their
daily/periodic regimen is shown to greatly improve health outcomes.
Expanding the cues, especially digital cues, patients receive to take
their medication would likely further improve adherence.

The addition of PILL BOX (OBJECT) would help medical professionals
communicate to many patients a low-cost and effective solution to a
serious problem in healthcare. The accessibility of pill boxes improves
health outcomes and granting medical professionals a simple symbol for
the tool will allow for greater access to a simple solution.

## Image Distinctiveness

The PILL BOX (OBJECT) is distinct from other container emojis, such as
the URN (OBJECT) or WRAPPED GIFT (ACTIVITIES), as it consists of several
smaller sections. Pill boxes have a standard and easily identifiable
shape and style.

## Completeness

The PILL BOX (OBJECT) does help in completeness. The PILL (OBJECT) is
the closest existing emoji to the purpose of the pill box, but there is
precedent for emojis that contain or deliver another object represented
by an emoji. The SYRINGE (OBJECT) and DROP OF BLOOD (OBJECT) and the
TAKEOUT BOX (FOOD & DRINK) and multiple food emojis both have similar
relationships to the pill box and the pill emoji. For people prescribed
pills, pill boxes are often recommended to regiment and remind them of
their dosage schedule, a relationship that warrants expression in emoji
form.

# Selection Factors – Exclusion

## Petitions or Frequently Requested

Not Applicable.

## Overly Specific

Pill boxes are used by patients with a wide variety of ailments and are
not specific to a subgroup. Pill boxes are also recommended to patients
for multiple reasons, as they can help reduce dosage discrepancy for
patients suffering from memory loss problems and patients with multiple
periodic doses.

## Open-ended

The introduction of PILL BOX emoji will not lead to the introduction of
an infinite set of emoji as the pill box has few alternatives in the
medical field. The medical relationship between the PILL BOX (OBJECT)
and PILL (OBJECT) is more explicit than that of other medical devices
and medicine, meaning the addition of more emojis under similar claims
is unlikely.

## Already Representable

The current crop of medical emojis is limited and does not represent the
diverse tools medical professionals have to ensure patient safety. On
its own, the PILL (OBJECT) emoji does not convey the regimented and
often complex habit of pill-taking in a way the PILL BOX (OBJECT) could.

## Logos, brands, UI icons, signage, specific people, specific landmarks, deities 

There are no logos, brands, deities, specific people or landmarks
represented by this.

## Transient

Pill boxes have been used for centuries, but have been relatively stable
in appearance for decades. The strength of the pill box is it’s
low-tech, affordable design. As more technologically advanced products
are introduced, it is unlikely the design of the pill box will change.
The most recognizable pill boxes feature seven compartments, each
representing a day of the week, and that model will likely persist even
as innovative features like auto-lock mechanisms, auditory reminders, or
digital compatibility are introduced.

## Faulty Comparison

We are comparing it mostly to the PILL (OBJECT), which is an appropriate
benchmark emoji to compare it to, so it is not faulty. We believe the
PILL BOX (OBJECT) strengthens the ability of medical professionals and
patients to use the PILL emoji.

## Exact Images

PILL BOXES (OBJECT) are recognizable by their shape and multiple
compartment features. This allows for variation in color (some pill
boxes are color-coded by day) and compartment number, while still being
recognizable as a pill box. This proposal does not require a specific
representation.
