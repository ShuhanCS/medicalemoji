# Abstract

We are requesting the addition of a STOMACH (ORGAN) emoji. The STOMACH
(ORGAN) is an organ in the upper abdomen that aids in food digestion by
secreting enzymes and acids to break down food. Without thinking, our
stomach is constantly working to digest the last meal we ate and pass
along the food to the small intestine, where our body absorbs the food’s
nutrients. Additionally, the stomach is widely seen as a symbol of our
appetite and weight loss, presenting an emoji with applicability outside
of the medical community.

The salience and importance of the stomach to the human body highlights
the usefulness of a stomach emoji for medical professionals and the
broader public.

# Introduction

The stomach is one of the most salient internal organs to the broader
public. Closely associated with gastrointestinal issues and consuming
food, the stomach’s role in the human body is widely understood.
Depending on culture and access to food, humans often eat anywhere from
2-4 meals a day, meaning our stomach is constantly working to digest
foods. Unsurprisingly, such an important organ also has many issues. The
top ten related queries to the search “stomach” all relate to some
medical issue a person experiences in their stomach.

*[figure: image5.png]**[figure: image3.png]*

The frequency at which people search for medical help on their stomach
suggests the emojification of the stomach would aid medical
professionals in communicating diagnoses.

Some of the most common pains and illnesses young children get are in
their digestive system. While often a form of constipation, stomach and
other digestive issues in young children can be serious, especially
considering the difficulty children have in reporting their symptoms.
One survey found that children ages three to five were able to
communicate thoughts exclusively using emojis. The survey also suggested
young children communicate using emojis in a unique way, meaning
expanding their visual vocabulary could help them relay their emotions
and feelings in a clear manner. Not just children, but those hindered by
language barriers or speech impediments could have a higher quality
medical experience with the addition of the stomach emoji.

Current emoji options feature dozens of foods which our stomachs digest
on a frequent basis, but do not offer the organ processing of all these
foods.

*[figure: image9.png]*

The introduction of a stomach emoji would be helpful to relay important
messaging about the ways these foods interact with our stomach and
digestive system. Being able to visually convey how a PEANUT (FOOD)
upsets the stomach of those with peanut allergies, or educating children
on how eating APPLES (FOOD) aids in stomach digestion would expand the
ages at which health professionals can convey to children proper eating
habits.

The addition of the STOMACH (ORGAN) would help reduce communication
barriers in the medical space between professionals and patients, help
transition medical communication to tele-health trends, and improve
overall public health.

### **Multiple Usages**

Stomachs are closely associated with food and eating. While the stomach
is one part of the larger digestive system, it is more closely
associated with feelings of hunger or feelings of fullness than other
parts of the digestive tract. For example, in certain cultures, the
feeling of the stomach “grumbling” connotes hunger, whereas the
expansion of the stomach is associated with fullness from eating.

Sometimes, the “stomach” is colloquially used to refer to the human
abdomen, such as to “lay down on your stomach”, meaning lay face down.

Many cuisines feature the stomach lining of farm animals as a delicacy.
Often called tripe, the meat features in dishes in Mexico (menudo), the
Philippines (paklay), India (chakna), Vietnam (pho), and many other
countries. Beyond the centrality of tripe in many dishes, tripe has many
health benefits, including high levels of zinc and vitamin B12.

## Image Distinctiveness

The ubiquity and distinctiveness of the STOMACH (ORGAN) makes it
differentiable from other internal organs and its simple structure makes
it distinct at an emoji scale.

## Completeness

The stomach is an integral part of the human body. The current crop of
internal organ emojis are limited and include no part of the digestive
system, a process central to human functioning.

# Selection Factors – Exclusion

## Petitions or Frequently Requested

Not Applicable.

## Overly Specific

The STOMACH (ORGAN) is not overly specific as all humans and many
animals have one.

## Open-ended

The STOMACH (ORGAN) is one of the most known and important internal
organs. As such, the emojification of the stomach would not lead to many
more emojis given the limited number of internal organs and the even
fewer number of organs with the salience of the stomach.

## Already Representable

As mentioned, there is no current emoji that predominantly features in
the digestive system. There are other internal organs emojis, but the
current number do not reflect the complexity and diversity of the human
body functions. Furthermore, none of the existing emojis closely
resemble the look of the stomach.

## Logos, brands, UI icons, signage, specific people, specific landmarks, deities 

There are no logos, brands, deities, specific people or landmarks
represented by this.

## Transient

As the STOMACH (ORGAN) is an organ in the human body, it will be
distinct in the future. The stomach can serve as a representation of the
digestive system and feature in medical and educational teachings for
the foreseeable future.

## Faulty Comparison

This proposal for the KIDNEY (ORGAN) emoji is submitted on its own
merits with no argument made due to exclusion of existing emoji.

## Exact Images

The stomach has a distinct general shape, but otherwise allows for
variation in representation.
