# Abstract

This proposal requests the addition of a KIDNEY (ORGAN) emoji. The
KIDNEYS (ORGAN) are two bean-shaped organs, each about the size of a
fist, located just below the rib cage, one on each side of your spine.
Healthy kidneys filter about a half cup of blood every minute, removing
waste and extra water to make urine.

The kidneys are known for being critical to the act of urination. Other
key functions performed by the kidneys include: balancing our PH level;
balancing our water; balancing our electrolyte levels; removing toxins
from our body; controlling blood pressure; help produce red blood cells;
help produce Vitamin D.

This highlights an important opportunity to improve public health by
making available a kidney emoji for digital communication.

# Introduction

Most people know that a major function of the kidneys is to remove waste
products and excess fluid from the body through urine. This fact alone
renders the kidneys a core part of not only human health, but of our
conscious lives. By some estimates, people spend on average 29 days and
5 hours in one lifetime urinating. Most people urinate between six and
eight times a day. For those hydrating abundantly, it's not abnormal to
go as many as 10 times a day. People may also pee more often if taking
certain medications, like diuretics for high blood pressure.

However, kidneys are also a critical piece of public and private
dialogue about human health. Each year, more than half a million people
go to emergency rooms for kidney stone problems. It is estimated that
one in ten people will have a kidney stone at some time in their lives.
The prevalence of kidney stones in the United States increased from 3.8%
in the late 1970s to 8.8% in the late 2000s. It has been said that
passing a kidney stone is the closest a man will get to experiencing the
pain of labor and childbirth. “Kidney stone pain can be very severe, and
many patients report it as the worst they have ever experienced,”
according to Mike Nguyen, MD, urologist at USC Urology of Keck Medicine
of USC and associate professor of clinical urology at the Keck School of
Medicine of USC. Thus kidneys may also be the organ associated with one
of the most memorable experiences of a person’s life.

In addition, Urinary Tract Infections account for over 6 million patient
visits to physicians in the U.S., with approximately 20% of these to the
emergency department. In such severe cases, UTIs that go untreated, may
travel from the urethra or bladder, to one or both kidneys. Without
prompt medical attention, and proper treatment, such UTI’s can
permanently damage the kidneys. Worse, the bacteria can spread to the
bloodstream and cause a life-threatening infection.

Moreover, the kidneys are unique organs in that most people are born
with two, and can also lead normal lives with just one. This thus makes
it more feasible and likely for people to donate a kidney to someone in
need. But in 2016, there were 100,791 people awaiting kidney
transplants, and the median wait time for an individual’s first kidney
transplant was 3.6 years. In 2014, 17,107 kidney transplants took place
in the US. Of these, 11,570 came from deceased donors and 5,537 came
from living donors. On average, over 3,000 new patients are added to the
kidney waiting list each month, which means that every 14 minutes,
someone is added to the kidney transplant list. 13 people die each day
while waiting for a life-saving kidney transplant, and in 2014, 4,761
patients died while waiting for a kidney transplant. Another 3,668
people became too sick to receive a kidney transplant.

Reducing barriers to open discussions of the risk factors, symptoms, and
patient impact of kidney disease has the potential to raise global
awareness.

# Selection Factors – Exclusion

## Overly Specific

The KIDNEY (ORGAN) is not overly specific as it is an organ universal to
all humans and to many other animals.

## Open Ended

The KIDNEY (ORGAN) is not only part of the body, but one of its most
important organs and therefore does not create an open-ended need for
new BODY PARTS.

## Already Representable

There is currently no other emoji that could represent or approximate
the KIDNEY (ORGAN), which was not the case for HEART (ORGAN) or BRAIN
(ORGAN).

## Logos, brands, UI icons, signage, specific people, deities 

The KIDNEY (ORGAN) represents no logos, brands, icons, signage, deities,
or specific people.

## Transient

The KIDNEY (ORGAN) is an essential organ in the human body and can thus
expect stable, long-term usage. Because the KIDNEY (ORGAN) can be used
representatively of the urinary system, it will continue to have a place
in its relation to health and science.

## Faulty Comparison

This proposal for the KIDNEY (ORGAN) emoji was submitted on its own
merits with no argument made due to the inclusion of existing emoji.

## Exact Images

The KIDNEY (ORGAN) conforms to a general shape and color, but does not
require an overly specific representation.
