# Abstract

This proposal requests the addition of a LIVER (ORGAN) emoji. The LIVER
(ORGAN) is one of the most iconic organs in the body and occupies a
critical role in the digestive system. Though there are a plethora of
food emoji, none represent the liver, the largest organ in the body. It
is also a vital organ for digesting food, storing energy, cleaning
blood, and fighting infection.

Worldwide, liver diseases have been growing. And though they can be
inherited, they are, for the most part, preventable. A variety of
factors may damage the liver, such as viruses, alcohol use and obesity.
Over time, conditions that damage the liver can lead to scarring
(cirrhosis), which can lead to liver failure, a life-threatening
condition. At the same time, early treatment may give the liver time to
heal.

This highlights an important opportunity to improve public health by
making available a liver emoji for digital communication.

# Introduction

Liver disease accounts for approximately 2 million deaths per year
worldwide. Cirrhosis, in which the liver does not function due to
long-term damage, is currently the 11th most common cause of death
globally and liver cancer is the 16th leading cause of death; combined,
they account for 3.5% of all deaths worldwide. Recent years have seen an
upward trend; between 2000 and 2016, liver cancer deaths were up 43
percent for men and 40 percent for women aged 25 and older, according to
the Center for Disease Control (CDC).

Most cases of liver disease are preventable, and are frequently
associated with alcohol and drug consumption, and with obesity and
diabetes.

For instance, alcohol is the main cause of liver-related death in
Europe, even with the possibility of underestimation of mortality due to
legal issues of documenting alcohol as the primary cause of death.
Meanwhile, Asian countries such as India have seen alcohol emerging as
the most common cause of chronic liver disease.

In another example, hepatitis is the inflammation of the liver that is
commonly caused by viral infection. Worldwide, hepatitis (especially
hepatitis B and hepatitis C) was responsible for 1.34 million deaths in
2015. The World Health Organization (WHO) also reports that deaths from
hepatitis have increased 22 percent since 2000. Yet vaccination is now
available for both hepatitis A and hepatitis B, and there are also drug
treatments for those who become infected.

Reducing barriers to open discussions of the risk factors, symptoms, and
patient impact of liver disease has the potential to raise global

## Overly Specific

The LIVER (ORGAN) is not overly specific as it is an organ universal to
all humans and to many other animals.

## Open Ended

The LIVER (ORGAN) is not only part of the body, but one of its most
important organs and therefore does not create an open-ended need for
new BODY PARTS.

## 

## Already Representable

No. There is currently no other emoji that could represent or
approximate the LIVER (ORGAN), which was not the case for HEART (ORGAN)
or BRAIN (ORGAN).

expect stable, long-term usage. Because the LIVER (ORGAN) can be used
representatively for the entire digestive system, it will continue to
