# The two proposals that won

The only documents this project ever placed in Unicode's public register, retrieved 2026-07-09.

| Document | Title | Source field, as registered | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| [`L2/19-149`](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2019/19149-lung-emoji.pdf) | `Proposal for Emoji: LUNG` | `Emojination / Christian Kamkoff, Shuhan He` | Encoded, `U+1FAC1`, Emoji 13.0 |
| [`L2/19-150`](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2019/19150-heart-emoji.pdf) | `Proposal for Emoji: HEART (ORGAN)` | `Emojination / Christian Kamkoff, Shuhan He` | Encoded, `U+1FAC0`, Emoji 13.0 |

Both are archived here as PDF and as extracted text. Both were submitted 2019-04-23, drafted 2019-03-30,
by Christian Kamkoff, Shuhan He MD (Massachusetts General Hospital) and Melissa Thermidor (NHS Blood and
Transplant).

## Why this folder exists

Compare these against the fifteen drafts in
[`../archive-2020-emojination-drafts/`](../archive-2020-emojination-drafts/). Every one of those was
declined. Every one of these was encoded. The differences are small, specific and worth copying.

### 1. Keywords

| | Keywords as filed |
|---|---|
| **Lung, won** | `breath, inhalation, exhalation, respiration` |
| **Heart, won** | `heartbeat, pulse, center` |
| **Kidney, lost** | `Kidney` |

Unicode's rule is explicit: *"Do not repeat the name of the emoji."* The kidney draft's only keyword is the
name of the emoji.

And the winners' keywords were adopted verbatim. The Emoji Subcommittee's recommendation `L2/19-190R`
records `U+1FAC0 HEART … heartbeat | pulse | center | organ` and `U+1FAC1 LUNGS … breath | inhalation |
exhalation | respiration | organ`. Unicode took the words straight off the proposal.

### 2. Evidence sources

Both winners cite Google Search, Google Trends twice, **Google Books Ngram Viewer**, and Bing. Neither
cites Instagram.

Of the fifteen 2020 drafts: **Ngram appears in three.** Instagram appears in four.

The Ngram Viewer is a mandated source today, and it was already in the winning proposals in 2019. Twelve
of the later drafts dropped a source the successful proposals had used.

### 3. Image rights, stated on the page

> Lung: *"Credit: Anna Zeng. Free for use in conjunction with this proposal"*
> Heart: *"Credit: Anna Zeng/Emojination Free for use in conjunction with this proposal. Black and white
> images pending finalization of color"*

A named artist, an explicit licence grant, and an acknowledgement that black and white artwork was still
outstanding. Today a missing licence is an automatic rejection.

### 4. What the winners did NOT do differently

They asked for the same classification the losers did:

> `Sort Order: in the BODY PARTS category, after BRAIN`

Both of them. So the `body-parts` placement that the [Health category
document](../utc-health-category/health-category-utc-doc.md) now asks the UTC to revisit was requested by
the winning proposals themselves. There was nowhere else for an organ to go. That is the argument, and our
own successful proposals are the evidence for it.

They also both contain a `Frequently Requested` section, which was part of Unicode's template in 2019 and
is now explicitly disallowed. So that section is not what killed the later drafts.

## The lesson

The winners had a named artist with a licence, keywords that were not the emoji's name, the full set of
frequency sources including Ngram, and an organization on the source line. The losers had a shorter
evidence base, an organization named only in the filename, and no route into the register.
