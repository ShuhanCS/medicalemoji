# Draft email to David Rhew

**From:** Shuhan He
**To:** David Rhew
**Subject:** Medical Emoji proposals for Microsoft review

Hi David,

I am attaching a revised Medical Emoji review packet that can be read without prior project context:

1. a short deck showing the full proposal portfolio and the current filing plan;
2. a brief explaining the individual proposal route and the separate UTC discussion-document route;
3. a page of technical and artwork-rights questions for Microsoft reviewers;
4. a UTC discussion draft on health-related emoji coverage; and
5. working proposal PDFs for CT Scan, Blood Bag, and Pill Box.

I currently plan to submit CT Scan and Blood Bag through Unicode's 2026 Emoji Submission Form after updating their evidence, artwork-ownership language, and document metadata. Pill Box is the first alternate. The filing window closes July 31, so this is late in the cycle, but it is still within Unicode's published window.

The deck now shows the other available concepts, including Pill Pack, IV Bag, Weight Scale, Ultrasound, and the remaining drafts. Pill Pack is not in the current plan because Web Trends evidence is missing, its Image Trends chart uses the wrong comparator, and the 18-pixel image can resemble a keypad. It also overlaps conceptually with Pill Box. IV Bag is missing both Trends exhibits and is difficult to distinguish from Blood Bag in black and white.

Could Microsoft help with two separate questions?

First, after I submit the selected proposal PDFs through the official form, would Microsoft's Unicode standards representatives be willing to ask the Emoji Standard & Research Working Group to consider adding them to an upcoming agenda as part of its normal review process? I understand that Unicode will control the timing and outcome.

Second, would Microsoft's standards team review the attached health-coverage discussion draft and, if it agrees that the question merits UTC discussion, help revise it for submission? Microsoft could submit a revised document, coauthor one with me, or use the draft as source material for its own document. The paper is separate from the individual encoding proposals, which would each go through the official form.

Related history may help frame the request. In 2019, Christian Kamkoff, Shuhan He, and Melissa Thermidor submitted separate proposals for Anatomical Heart and Lungs; Unicode later encoded both characters. Apple's L2/18-080 presented nine related accessibility emoji as a finite starting set in 2018. Apple used the emoji-proposal process available at that time. Under the 2026 process, each encoding proposal must use the submission form; a separate technical document can raise the broader coverage question.

If you think these questions are worth pursuing, could you help identify the appropriate Microsoft standards and emoji-design reviewers? I would also appreciate your view on whether CT Scan and Blood Bag are the right filing choices, or whether Pill Box should replace one of them.

Thank you,

Shuhan
