# Email to Clifford Hudis (CMSS / ASCO)

**To:** Clifford.hudis@asco.org
**From:** Shuhan He, MD
**Subject:** Following up on medical emoji — what a CMSS letter would unlock

---

Dear Dr. Hudis,

Thank you again for taking the time to talk through the medical emoji effort. You were generous and open to it, and you mentioned wanting to understand the work more fully before deciding whether the Council of Medical Specialty Societies might lend its voice. This note, and the short briefing attached, are meant to give you exactly that — the evidence, the precedent, and a specific, low-lift ask.

**The precedent.** In 2020, our team helped add the first anatomically accurate organ emoji — the anatomical heart (🫀) and the lungs (🫁) — to the Unicode Standard. They now render on essentially every smartphone and platform on Earth. That win was covered by the [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2020/02/04/metro/this-mass-general-doctor-helped-get-two-new-medical-emojis-approved/) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2021/9/13/22665002/doctors-medical-emoji-organs-health), recognized by [Harvard Medical School](https://hms.harvard.edu/news/emoji-power), and the clinical rationale was published in [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/2783847), with companion letters in *Hepatology* and *The Spine Journal*.

**What we are building now.** A second, larger slate — **kidney, liver, stomach, spine, white blood cell, and EKG** — plus a comprehensive medical emoji reference at [medicalemoji.org](https://medicalemoji.org). The longer-term goal is to get the Unicode Consortium to treat clinical iconography as a category worth maintaining, rather than approving organ symbols one hard-won submission at a time.

**Why CMSS.** The Unicode process rewards two things: evidence of widespread real-world usage, and credible signals that recognized authorities consider a symbol important and well-defined. We have built the first. Organized medicine is the natural source of the second. We already hold letters from the American College of Emergency Physicians (a project-wide endorsement of all ten concepts), the American Gastroenterological Association, the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy, the American Society of Nephrology, the International Society of Nephrology, the National Kidney Foundation, and roughly a dozen more. What no single specialty society can provide is what CMSS uniquely represents: the unified voice of the house of medicine across specialties.

**The ask.** One letter, signed at the Council level, that (1) affirms the clinical-communication and health-literacy value of a standardized set of medical emoji, and (2) encourages Unicode to consider them and to engage organized medicine as a partner on future clinical symbols. It does not commit CMSS to drafting proposals, funding, or ongoing work — that is on us. The attached briefing includes a model paragraph your team can edit freely.

I would be glad to present this to the appropriate CMSS committee or get on a short call with whomever you'd like to involve. Whatever pace and process works for the Council works for us.
