## The ask — one thing

A single **AMIA letter of support to the Unicode Consortium**, modeled on the one ACEP wrote,
affirming the value of a standardized set of medical emoji. It commits AMIA to nothing else — we
handle the Unicode proposals, the usage evidence, and the artwork. **A turnkey draft letter is
attached.**

## The 6 symbols in our August 2026 Unicode submission

| Symbol | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Kidney** | Chronic kidney disease affects ~10% of adults. |
| **Liver** | ~2M deaths/year; NAFLD in ~25% of US adults. |
| **Stomach** | GI disease, GERD, ulcers. |
| **Spine** | Low back pain — #1 cause of disability worldwide. |
| **White blood cell** | Infection, immunity, sepsis. |
| **EKG** | Cardiovascular disease — the #1 killer. |

## We have done this before

In 2020 our physician team added the first anatomically accurate **heart (🫀)** and
**lungs (🫁)** to the Unicode Standard. They now render on essentially every phone — Apple
(iOS 14.2), Google, Samsung, Microsoft. This is the proven next slate, not a concept pitch.

**The evidence is published:** *JAMA* (2021, the clinical case), *JAMA* (2022, an emoji pain
scale that matched the standard numeric scale), and *JAMA Network Open* (2023, the case for a
comprehensive set). Covered by the *Boston Globe* and *The Verge*; *STAT* (Jan 2026) found emoji
rising fast in clinical records.

## Organized medicine already backs it

ACEP (the whole project), AGA, ASGE, ASN, ISN, NKF, KDIGO, AAKP, and a dozen more. What is still
missing is the voice of **organized informatics**. That is AMIA.

## Why this is AMIA's lane

Medical emoji are **standardized clinical iconography** — a controlled-vocabulary,
interoperability, and patient-communication problem. Unicode rewards two things: real-world usage
(we build that) and a recognized authority affirming a symbol matters and is well-defined. The
national informatics body is the ideal source of the second.
