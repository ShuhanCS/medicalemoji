# Who can help route the Medical Emoji work

This is the accessible text companion to the three-page contact map:

https://github.com/ShuhanCS/medicalemoji/blob/codex/eligible-2026-slate/output/pdf/2026-07-13-who-can-help-with-medical-emoji-review.pdf

The public roles below were checked against official Unicode sources on July 13, 2026. Unicode does not
publish Microsoft's internal delegate assignments or a complete current roster for the Emoji Standard &
Research Working Group, so those roles should be confirmed inside Microsoft.

## Recommended route for David Rhew

Start with **Peter Constable** for the technical and standards path. He is a Microsoft employee, Unicode
Technical Vice President, and chair of the Unicode Technical Committee. The latest completed UTC minutes
also list him as representing Microsoft.

Use **Vishal Chowdhary** for executive routing. He is Microsoft's representative on the Unicode Board of
Directors and can help identify the current Microsoft owners. The Board does not select emoji or decide
technical outcomes.

The practical route is:

`David Rhew -> Peter Constable -> current Microsoft member representative and delegates -> UTC leadership`

Vishal can help make or confirm the Microsoft introductions. Peter or Vishal should identify:

1. Microsoft's primary Unicode Organizational Member Representative;
2. the primary UTC delegate and alternate;
3. Microsoft's ESR delegate or participant, if one is designated;
4. the Fluent Emoji, Segoe UI, font-engineering, and accessibility owners; and
5. a legal or IP reviewer only if Microsoft contributes artwork, coauthors a paper, or approves an
   implementation statement.

## Two separate submission routes

### Individual emoji proposals

Each selected proposal must be submitted separately through Unicode's 2026 Emoji Submission Form. Emailing
a proposal to a Unicode or Microsoft contact does not submit it.

`Final proposal PDF -> 2026 Emoji Submission Form -> ESR review -> UTC action`

Jennifer Daniel chairs ESR. Ned Holbrook is vice-chair of both ESR and UTC in Unicode's current central
leadership directory. ESR reviews proposals and develops recommendations; UTC retains authority over
technical decisions.

Official guidance:

https://www.unicode.org/emoji/proposals.html

Official 2026 form:

https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSesdtPEbXCxXQnOb34UwhK7yPuCk52Pqix4FfQYgmW9Kt5cAw/viewform?usp=send_form

### Health-related emoji coverage paper

The UTC-format paper follows a separate document route. It does not submit an emoji and cannot replace the
form above.

`Authors confirm -> UTC document channel and agenda request -> UTC discussion -> possible ESR referral`

The authors should confirm the final text and public authorship before filing. A normal document submission
goes to `docsubmit@unicode.org` with the subject `UTC Doc: Health-related emoji coverage`. A Unicode member
should also request a corresponding agenda item from the UTC chairs with `UTC Agenda` in the subject.

UTC #188 is scheduled for July 28-30, 2026. Unicode says the ordinary document deadline is the Monday before
the meeting, July 20, but it also warns that specialized review can require more lead time. Microsoft's
standards lead should advise whether the July meeting is realistic or whether the next meeting is the better
route.

Official document process:

https://www.unicode.org/pending/docsubmit.html

UTC meeting information:

https://www.unicode.org/L2/meetings/utc-meetings.html
