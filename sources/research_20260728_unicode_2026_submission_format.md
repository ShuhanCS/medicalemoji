# Unicode 2026 Emoji Submission Format - Verified Requirements

Research date: 2026-07-28

Primary source: https://www.unicode.org/emoji/proposals.html

Source last updated: 2026-05-20

Intake window: 2026-04-02 through end of day 2026-07-31.

## Required proposal structure

1. Title: `Proposal for Emoji: <name>`.
2. Submitter names separated with semicolons, one main point of contact, and an updated revision date.
3. Identification with suggested keywords and category. Keywords should not repeat the proposed emoji name.
4. Color and black-and-white example images at 18x18 and 72x72 pixels at the top of page one. Grayscale is
   not acceptable.
5. Image-rights and licensing certification.
6. Factors for Inclusion: Multiple meanings; Use in sequences; Breaks new ground; Distinctiveness; Expected
   usage/frequency; Completeness; Compatibility.
7. Factors for Exclusion: Already representable; Overly specific; Open-ended; Transient; Faulty comparison.
8. Other information.
9. A publicly accessible PDF submitted through the Unicode Emoji Submission Form.

## Required frequency evidence

The proposal must contain publicly available, reproducible screenshots from:

1. Google Search, including the result count.
2. Google Video Search, including the result count.
3. Google Trends Web Search, with `elephant` as the comparison and the widest practical range.
4. Google Trends Image Search, with `elephant` as the comparison and the widest practical range.
5. Google Books Ngram Viewer, with `elephant` as the comparison and the widest practical range.

Search results are acknowledged to be time-specific snapshots. Petitions, calls for the emoji, and anecdotal
evidence are not acceptable substitutes for empirical frequency data.

## Compatibility boundary

The formal 2026 Compatibility factor asks whether the emoji is needed for compatibility with a popular existing
system, such as Snapchat, X, or QQ. A positive claim must include screenshots and evidence of high-frequency use
in that system. The guidelines instruct submitters to mark this factor `n/a` unless compelling evidence exists.

The Kidney proposal's CLDR mismatch is still useful under Breaks new ground and Already representable. Without
named-system screenshots and frequency evidence, it should not be presented as satisfying the formal 2026
Compatibility factor.
